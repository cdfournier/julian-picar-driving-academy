# STOP Sign Circle

STOP Sign Circle teaches repeatable turning, orientation discipline, and recovery inside an imperfect room.

The goal is not a perfect mathematical circle. The goal is a durable pattern:

```text
turn, acquire, continue, recover
```

## Why This Lesson Exists

Drivers often want every target centered before moving on. That can feel precise, but every little correction changes the car's path. A correction that improves the camera frame may push the car outside the useful arc.

STOP Sign Circle teaches drivers to prefer permanence over perfection. A target that is readable is often good enough. The lesson is to keep a direction, acquire the next sign, and continue without decorative corrections.

## Setup

Place four readable STOP signs around the car, roughly one per quadrant.

The signs do not need to sit at perfect compass points. Imperfect placement is useful because real driving targets are rarely mathematical.

Use a safe open room with enough floor for arcing turns. Avoid first runs near stairs, water, fragile objects, or tight furniture pockets.

## Level 1: Four Acquisitions

Goal: turn in one direction and acquire all four signs.

Prompt:

```text
You are practicing STOP Sign Circle.

SETUP:
  Four STOP signs are placed around the car.
  They are not perfectly aligned.

GOAL:
  Keep turning in one direction.
  Find each STOP sign.
  Count a sign as acquired when it is readable.
  Do not chase perfect centering.

RULES:
  Use committed arcs.
  Read camera and distance after each arc.
  If a STOP sign appears near the edge, count it if readable.
  If low geometry tightens, stop and recover.
```

Graduates when: the driver can find all four signs without panic-switching direction or over-correcting every frame.

## Level 2: Fewer Moves

Goal: reduce hesitation while preserving safety.

Prompt:

```text
You are practicing STOP Sign Circle: fewer moves.

GOAL:
  Acquire all four STOP signs with fewer body moves than last time.
  Use bold arcs to acquire.
  Use small corrections only for safety or readability.

REMEMBER:
  Readable is enough.
  Small corrections change the whole path.
  Boldness is not recklessness.
```

Graduates when: the driver can reduce unnecessary moves while still noticing low obstacles and recovering cleanly.

## Level 3: Four-Move Benchmark

Goal: explore whether each committed arc can carry the car from one STOP sign to the next.

This is a benchmark, not a requirement. Do not turn the lesson into stunt driving. A four-move circle is interesting only if it remains safe, repeatable, and teachable.

Graduates when: the driver understands the relationship between steering angle, speed, duration, traction, and turn radius well enough to explain why a move worked or failed.

## Recovery Pattern

If a bold arc carries the car into low-obstacle geometry:

```text
stop
look
backtrack along the curve
look again
continue when the lane is legible
```

Retracing is not failure. It preserves the lesson by returning the car to a readable state.

## Operator Notes

- Imperfect sign placement is acceptable and useful.
- Do not require a centered sign if the sign is readable.
- Confirm acquisitions when the driver is learning what counts.
- Warn about low furniture, chair bases, stands, and table legs.
- If boldness creates a worse path, name the tradeoff instead of scolding.
- Success is repeatable orientation, not a pretty screenshot.

## Driver Notes

- Pick one turn direction and keep it.
- Use bold arcs to make the room change.
- Count readable signs.
- Avoid decorative corrections.
- Respect low geometry more than open sensor readings.
- If the path tightens, recover by retracing.
- Seek permanence, not perfection.
